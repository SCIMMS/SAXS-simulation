Fast Surface-Mesh Projected-Density Simulation for Low-q Single-Shot XFEL Diffraction: Validation, Benchmarking, and Public-Data Template Retrieval

Synopsis

A surface-mesh projected-density kernel is validated for fast low-q single-shot XFEL forward simulation and tested with analytical references, performance benchmarks, and public CXIDB nanoparticle and GroEL data.

Abstract

Rapid low-q forward simulation is useful for morphology screening, orientation-library construction, and candidate-model testing in single-shot XFEL diffraction, but analytical form factors are limited to ideal shapes and dense voxel or three-dimensional FFT approaches can be expensive when only a detector-plane region is required. We present a sample-level method that represents particles as oriented triangular surface meshes. For each orientation, signed surface-depth rasterization produces a projected-density image, and a chirp z-transform samples low-q detector-plane amplitudes without constructing a full reciprocal-space volume. The implementation was validated using spheres, ellipsoids, boxes, polyhedra, and scalar-field objects. These tests checked projected-density accuracy, projected-mass conservation, mesh-resolution convergence, and sampling. Representative projected-density and intensity errors were at or below the 10^-3 level, and the analytical box comparison gave a relative intensity error of 4.07 x 10^-5. Benchmarks confirmed the reduction from dense O(N^3) voxel storage to projected-image O(N^2) storage plus compact mesh data. For CXIDB ID57 nanoparticle patterns, a fixed 240-orientation core-shell library recovered all synthetic queries and outperformed a radial-profile-preserving randomized control for all 209 public experimental frames, with median log-correlation scores of 0.724752 and 0.443121. A GroEL case converted public PDB structure 1SS8 into a smooth density grid, isosurface meshes, decimated assemblies, and a 429-template D7 library. Because the GroEL templates showed only a small advantage over a radial control, this case is interpreted as protein-density modeling and scalability rather than high-confidence orientation retrieval. The framework provides a reproducible, geometry-general kernel for low-q single-shot diffraction workflows.
